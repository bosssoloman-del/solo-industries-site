Revenue architecture

Four engines.
Built to settle fast.

The subsidiaries are what the company owns. These are how cash moves through it — engineered around the friction nobody mentions: payment holds, spam filters, and platform saturation.

Engine 01

B2B Automated Operation

Who it serves. Local trade businesses

The problem. Plumbers, HVAC techs, mechanics, roofers, tow operators miss inbound calls and form submissions while they are working — and lose $500 to $2,000 jobs to whoever answers first.

The product. A lightweight lead-forwarding service that pushes a web form straight to the owner’s cell as an SMS within five seconds of submission.

$150 – $300 flat per setup

Engine 02

Hyper-Niche Digital Products

Who it serves. 1099 contractors and night-shift trades

The problem. Independent operators run their books out of a shoebox and a phone camera, then lose the deductions at tax time.

The product. Hyper-specific operational sheets — the 1099 Tradesman Mileage & Expense Ledger and its siblings. Built once, sold repeatedly.

$15 – $30 per sale, automated

Engine 03

Impulse & Streetwear POD

Who it serves. Culture buyers and collectors

The problem. Niche culture has money and taste and almost nothing made specifically for it that is not watered down.

The product. High-impact prints and print-on-demand apparel — the PHAT Man tiers plus the capsule lines. Made after the order, shipped by the partner.

$20 – $50 profit per unit

Engine 04

Programmatic AI Gateway

Who it serves. Developers building agents

The problem. Autonomous agent builders need clean structured JSON endpoints and mostly get scraped HTML and rate limits.

The product. A FastAPI micro-service with a freemium tier — free calls, then metered. The Lab sells what the Lab already runs.

Metered, pay-per-call

Distribution

Pinterest is a search engine, not a feed

People arrive there with intent to buy or to curate. That makes it the top of the funnel for the digital-to-physical loop — not a place to post and hope.

Stage 1

The visual hook

A 4K wallpaper and matching keyboard skin shown on a phone mockup. It costs nothing to produce and it customizes the one screen people look at a hundred times a day.

Stage 2

Zero-friction magnet

One word in the comments triggers the automated delivery of the free pack. No form, no email gate, no funnel fatigue.

Stage 3

Physical monetization

Inside that free pack sits the link and a discount code for the matching physical piece — the heavyweight hoodie, the canvas print, the snapback.

Affiliate layer

The passive tail

Every free download confirmation carries a Recommended Stack — the tools actually used to build this company. Even when a visitor never buys a hoodie, a share of them go start their own store, and that recurs.

Hosting

The stack that runs the storefronts and the tunnels. Recurring commission.

Workspace

The documentation and planning layer behind the whole operation.

Print-on-demand

The fulfilment partner that makes inventory-free apparel possible in the first place.

Affiliate relationships are disclosed. SoLo Industries only recommends tools it uses to run its own lines.
